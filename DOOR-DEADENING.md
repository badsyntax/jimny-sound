# Door Sound Deadening — Plan

Butyl CLD mat on both front doors, fitted DIY. The fronts are in the kick panels, not the doors, so
this is about killing door-skin resonance, rattles and road noise — not baffling a door speaker.

## Material

- **Silent Coat 2mm** — butyl rubber with an aluminium constraining layer (alubutyl), **not bitumen**.
  Sheets are 375 × 250mm, about **3kg/m²**. Silent Coat say it's fine anywhere in the car, including
  under the bonnet.
- 2mm is the standard thickness — Dynamat Xtreme is 1.7mm. The 3mm and 4mm versions risk the door
  card not sitting flush.
- Considered and passed on: **Dynamat Xtreme** (≈2× the price, barely audible difference on a door)
  and **ResoNix** (best measured CLD, but not sold in the UK and $340 a box before shipping and import).
- Cheap foil-topped bitumen looks identical — the listing should say **butyl**.

## Coverage

CLD doesn't need 100%. Covering **25–50%** of the centre of a panel removes most of the resonance;
past that it's diminishing returns.

| Area | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Outer skin** (inside of the painted panel) | **25–50%**, centred on the largest flat area | The priority. Knuckle-tap test: hollow ring → dull thud |
| **Inner skin** (metal the door card clips to) | ~25%, flat areas that ring | Keep clear of clip holes, window channel, lock rods — and keep it **inside** the vapour barrier's sealant bead |
| Access holes | Not sealing | Only matters for door-mounted speakers |
| Rattles | As found | Silent Coat offcuts for metal-on-metal; fleece tape for wiring and plastic contact points |

At this coverage each door takes about a kilo. 16 sheets (≈1.5m²) does both doors with spare for
rattles; the rear quarters and tailgate can follow later.

## Door card removal (JB43)

Best photo guide: [BigJimny forum walkthrough](https://www.bigjimny.com/forum/6-jimny-chat/58049-door-card-internal-handle-removal-replacement).
Video: [ScegliAuto — pick the 1998–2018 JB43 one](https://www.scegliauto.com/en/video/suzuki/jimny/pannello-portiera/tutorial/).

1. **Inner release handle** — remove the screw, slide the unit towards the hinge to free two lugs,
   pull it out. Release the steel rod from its green clip with thin-nosed pliers.
2. **Plastic rivet** near the hinge end — press the centre in gently until it clicks (~3mm). Too hard
   and it drops inside the door.
3. **Pull handle** — pop the screw covers, remove the screws and handle.
4. **Lock knob** — unscrew it and tape the rod thread so it doesn't snag.
5. **Pop the card** — pull the bottom away with a trim tool: **7 press studs**. Lift it upwards (rear
   first); the top hooks over the windowsill lip.
6. **Window switch plug** — hold the barb down with a thin screwdriver; it's stubborn. Don't pull the wires.

Refit: test the window switches first, thump the studs home, pull-handle screws, rivet, release rod
and handle, lock knob. Recheck the windows and the door release.

Also worth checking while it's open: the lock rod tapping against the door metal is a known JB43 rattle.

## Vapour barrier (plastic sheet)

**Peel it, don't cut it.** It's held on by a bead of black butyl sealant that stays tacky.

- Warm it with a hairdryer, then peel slowly from a corner with a trim tool. Most of the sealant stays
  on the door — **leave it there, don't wipe it off**.
- Store the sheet flat, sticky side up.
- Refit onto the original bead and press firmly all round. Patch gaps in the bead with butyl tape,
  and small tears with tape. Check cables and rods still pass through their holes.
- Always refit it — it keeps water running down inside the door off the back of the door card.

## Fitting

1. Wind the window **fully down** before working on the outer skin.
2. Degrease with **IPA** on a lint-free cloth; let it flash off. **No rust-proofing wax** where the mat
   goes — wax on any bare metal or the mat edges only afterwards.
3. Cut the mat **before** peeling the backing, from the paper side, knife + metal ruler on cardboard.
   **Gloves** — the cut foil edges are sharp. Smaller pieces go through the access holes more easily.
4. Don't fit it when it's freezing. Roll it hard with the roller until the foil's embossed pattern
   flattens out — that's a full bond.
5. Keep the **drain holes** at the bottom of the door clear.

It is removable later — heat gun and plastic scraper. Messy, not impossible.
